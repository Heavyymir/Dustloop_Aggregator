Overview

Venom is baller—literally. No character in fighting games plays truly like Venom, thanks to his unique Pool Ball mechanic. He has two special moves—Summoning BallGuardStartup11Recovery15Advantage and Dubious CurveGuardMidStartup21Recovery19Advantage-3—which place pool balls on the screen. Venom launches these balls at the opponent by hitting them with attacks. These pool balls are so interwoven into the fabric of the character that every facet of his gameplay leads back to the balls somehow.

Venom's balls can be placed, moved, launched, re-launched, charged, lightning-infused, and teleported to. Learning to utilize this flexibility is the most rewarding and important aspect of the character. This gives Venom's play many moving parts which can be adjusted in real time, allowing a freeform approach to any given match. Mastery involves a firm grasp of fighting game fundamentals. In neutral, Venom's balls function as an extension of his normals. The trajectory and damage of a given ball is determined by the attack Venom hits it with, so a Venom player should be comfortable moving around his balls and using them to shut off lanes of space.

Beyond neutral, Venom has some of the stronger pressure and okizemeFrom Japanese "起き攻め". Attacking an opponent about to wake up after they were knocked down, usually with meaty attacks or mix-ups. in the game. Because balls are projectiles, they provide frame advantage while Venom acts and moves around the opponent, all while dealing chip damage. Venom's ideal knockdowns give him room to set up between 1 and 3 balls, or Enhanced Stinger AimGuardAllStartup6 after releaseRecovery30 after releaseAdvantagesee notes. From there, the world is his billiard table. He can enforce very scary high/low mixups with empty jump > low, late airdash > overhead, instant overheads, and F-ShikiSometimes known as "Fuzzy Overhead". When you are in blockstun, you can switch high/low blocking, but your blocking animation and hurtbox does not change until you leave blockstun or block another attack. F-Shikis take advantage of this and use overheads that would miss on crouching characters, but not on standing characters.. He can also run a decent left/right mixup by dash jumping over an opponent and airdashing back into them with j.S or empty airdash > throw, and he has a phenomenal throw game.

The main challenges Venom faces are related to the double Ds: Deterrance and Defense. In a game where some characters can deal 250+ damage off a single combo, Venom's comparatively low base damage means his player needs to succeed in more interactions than average. Venom may have trouble discouraging certain characters from gambling on high-risk, high-reward options because the reward for them landing it is higher than Venom's reward for preventing it. Additionally, Venom has absolutely no reversal options, so his players will need to become very confident with universal defensive mechanics.

Ultimately, though, these flaws are not enough to keep this baller down, but rather help mold Venom's unique identity with strong and weak points. Players can and do take Venom to the absolute highest level of competitive play, and are rewarded with an incredibly unique and stylish character.

Venom makes use of his balls to apply scary, chip-damage heavy pressure to the enemy player while up close and to zone while at long range.

Pros
Cons
  • Diverse Pressure Options: Venom's balls give him a long list of neutral and offensive options to suffocate opponents with. The depth of his pressure can allow a player who labs to deploy a myriad of varied and strong setups.
  • Your Meter or Your Life: Venom's offense is projectile based which forces opponents to eat large amounts of chip damage, or spend a lot of tension on FD. With meter to spend on Dark AngelGuardAllStartup7+18Recovery10Advantage and Stinger Aim FRC, there are situations where the opponent must respect him.
  • Pokes And Neutral Game: 2S, f.S, and Stinger AimGuardAllStartup13RecoveryTotal 37Advantage+2 are moves with few rivals in terms of meterless space control. Furthermore, all of his neutral options become much better when he has balls to pair them with.
  • Guard Bar Raiser: Moves like c.S, Carcass RaidGuardAllStartup16RecoveryTotal 34Advantage+8, and Stinger Aim FRC allow Venom to stick to the opponent and raise their guard bar quickly and consistently.
  • Menacing From The Air: Venom's air normals are great. Rising j.P catches many air approaches, and j.K is active for ages. His dive kickGuardHigh/AirStartup18Recovery6 after landingAdvantage+7, air ball setGuardStartup9Recovery26Advantage, and teleportGuardStartup15Recovery2Advantage open up lots of ways to change Venom’s trajectory, timing, and location, making him hard to pin down and anti-air.
  • Secret Grappler: Between his plus-on-block projectiles, strong tick throw normals, charge move feints, and his strong throw option select, Venom has a lot of ways to threaten with his best-in-class throw
  • Deterrence and Reward: Venom generally needs some combination of a guard bar, corner positioning, ball setups, and meter to deal damage comparable to many other members of the cast. Stray hits can offer as little as a ball set. Along these lines, Venom can struggle to discourage opponents from gambling on high-risk, high-reward options, and usually wins in a higher number of interactions.
  • Susceptible to Counter Hits: Most of Venom's specials leave him in counter hit state for extended periods. This opens up additional punish options for his opponents, and leads to stray hits hurting more than they otherwise would.
  • Limited Defense: Venom is the only character in the game without any form of reversal. He will be forced to hold pressure that other characters can contest.

Unique Mechanics

Pool Balls

214X places a ball on the screen and shifts other placed balls into position according to the predefined Ball Formations. All balls which are set begin in an inert state. Venom can teleport to the inert ball currently on screen which was placed first. Inert balls can be hit by Venom's normals, specials with hitboxes, and other balls. Dark Angel does not interact with balls, and Tactical ArchGuardStartup12Recovery17Advantage will override his inert balls.

The speed, angle, and damage of a given ball is determined by the move which launched the ball. As a general guideline, the damage and speed are directly related, and scale from "lightest" buttons to "heaviest" buttons. The basic idea, ignoring exceptions, is from lowest to highest: P>K>S>H>D. The angle follows a logical geometry related to the move that launched the ball. For example, 5P is a horizontal attack, and as such launched the ball horizontally. 6P is an attack diagonally upwards and forwards, and as such launches the ball in a similar angle.

c.S is one of Venom's most important normals. It allows him to launch opponents for combos, hit tricky anti-air angles, charge Stinger AimGuardAllStartup13RecoveryTotal 37Advantage+2 and Carcass RaidGuardAllStartup16RecoveryTotal 34Advantage+8, crank guard bar, bait bursts, and more.

c.S > [2]8SGuardAllStartup16RecoveryTotal 34Advantage+8 is one of Venom's most important pressure resets thanks to the frame advantage of [2]8S combined with the flexibility of c.S. Every Venom player needs to become comfortable with buffering a charge during c.S for this reason. Similarly, the jump cancel allows you to mix up by going into IAS j.236SGuardHigh/AirStartup18Recovery6 after landingAdvantage+7 for a fast and rewarding overhead mixup.

It's important to consider that c.S has a short proximity range, and as such you will often need to use dash momentum or air dash momentum to ensure that you are within proximity range after your block string/combo starter. Because the proximity range is slightly larger than his throw range, and because this is his fastest normal without a downwards input, this is one of the best choices to option select with throw.

Because this move starts the majority of Venom's combos and block strings, many players will attempt to burst it. Venom players should be prepared to use burst throw option selects, or read the opponent, jump cancel c.S and block or throw their burst. Doing so is one of the best ways to make the opponent fear you, and Venom loves every bit of respect he can get out of the opponent.

6P is used as an anti-air and occasional counter-poke, but also sees use in combos.

Due to a combination of issues with its range, reward on far-hit, and cancel options, 6P cannot function as a one-size-fits-all anti-air, and is at its best dealing with diagonal jump-ins and low airdashes, which help guarantee good positioning and a deep hit for better routing. 6P should be supplemented by c.SGuardMidStartup5Recovery20Advantage-10, rising j.PGuardHigh/AirStartup7Recovery9Advantage, j.DGuardHigh/AirStartup7Recovery13+6 after landingAdvantage, and Air ThrowGuard88 pixelsStartupRecoveryAdvantage for higher reward or greater situational reliability.

6P's reward as an anti-air varies fairly greatly depending on the spacing at which the opponent is hit, and which character the opponent is playing. Generally:

  • Deep air hits can combo into c.S for a full confirm fairly universally
  • Low and close hits can generally combo into Dubious Curve
    • This applies to large/heavy characters like Johnny, Potemkin, and Robo-Ky under most conditions
  • High and/or far air hits generally do not combo well and are best canceled into Ball Set, or FB Double Head Morbid if you absolutely need a knockdown
    • This is sometimes the case with light/floaty characters, who get hit too high to meaningfully convert on
  • Grounded hits can easily be routed into 5H > Dubious Curve or 2D for a knockdown

There are two key limitations to 6P that are important to be mindful of. One, its disjointed anti-air hitbox is only active for three frames. Two, Venom takes a step forward during the move's startup. As such, 6P can be fairly finnicky regarding spacing and timing, leading to struggles with matchup-defining jump-ins like Sol's j.HGuardHigh/AirStartup9Recovery0Advantage, Baiken's j.HGuardHigh/AirStartup9Recovery13Advantage, and May's j.2HGuardHigh/AirStartup13Recovery0Advantage. It can be important to dash or make use of the step forwards to 'project' 6P's hitbox into moves to help guarantee deep hits for more reliable anti-airing and better conversions.

The most notable strength is the close knockdown it gives on hit. 214X, known most commonly as Ball Set, is the move that defines Venom as a character.

Whenever Venom places an inert ball, either with 214X or with 421X, the ball will fall into formation relative to Venom. The most recently placed ball determines which formation is used. Force Break Carcass Raid is an under-explored move with novel applications for zoning.

Compared to [2]8H, which it is modeled after, [2]8D's projectile bounces more times, travels slower, and does not disappear if Venom is hit during its flight. Even though Force Break Carcass Raid shares its trajectory with [2]8H, it does not disappear if Venom gets hit, meaning it has some utility as a tool for trying to set up defensive trades in zoning sequences.

Dark Angel is Venom's ultimate lockdown tool, carrying opponents to the corner and forcing them to deal with a substantial meter-or-life decision:

  • Normal block: Deals roughly 12% chip damage on a full connect
  • Faultless Defense: Strips roughly 60% of the opponent's meter if fully FD'd

Much of Dark Angel's strength comes from just how long it's on screen; it gives Venom time to set a ball and jump for a high/low mixup, set two balls and keep the opponent blocking with a grounded normal, or run a mixup while visually obscured by the projectile. Alternatively, Dark Angel can be used to salvage bad/awkward knockdowns, or add extra option coverage and chip damage to existing setups.

As an Overdrive, Dark Angel doesn't inflict a tension penalty, letting Venom bolster or extend his offense while building meter for a future metered option. This allows for extended offensive sequences where the opponent faces a significantly more damaging conversion if they get hit, or the risk of getting Dark Angel'd again if they fail to escape.

Dark Angel can be used in guaranteed combos under some circumstances, such as for corner carry after a air hit 6K or throw, though it loses hits as the opponent 'floats' to the top of the projectile. It also has use in superflash unblockables, both as the source of superflash or as the projectile if you have 100 meter.

The game includes a hidden shortcut for the Dark Angel input, allowing you to perform the move with . The diagonal directional inputs are entirely optional for this move.

Tactical Arch is a niche and somewhat under-explored overdrive.

632146X sets 5 inert lightning balls in the formation corresponding to the button used to input the overdrive. This has 2 primary uses: long range chip damage setups, and super-freeze unblockables. The first of these cases is not only very niche, but also tends to be matchup specific, like for the Justice matchup. The chip damage isn't amazing because Tactical Arch maxes out at 15 hits, compared to Dark Angel's maximum of 28 hits.

More potently, Tactical Arch is used in super-freeze unblockable setups. This is done by setting a ball, hitting it (typically with 5P or 5K), and then activating Tactical Arch. Venom's projectiles will continue to travel during super freeze. This means that if the opponent is not already blocking when the super-freeze triggers they will get hit by any projectile that moved into them before the end of the super-freeze.

Notably, even though Tactical Arch's projectiles are created by a super they do not inherit super properties and, as such, do not lock out burst.
